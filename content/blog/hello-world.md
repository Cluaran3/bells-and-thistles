Five years ago I helped organize a strategic session on how we should popularize AI for the general public in a given country. In hindsight, it was a rather bizarre objective because neither AI itself needed any popularization besides its organic growth and development, nor the public opinion really mattered to any decision maker regarding this or any other significant issue. However, at that time both business representatives and public officials agreed that it would be nice to have a communication strategy addressing common fears and promoting positive effects of AI adoption. According to the survey conducted by the national statistics bureau, the two most popular fears/objections surfaced:
- AI will kill us all (with inevitable references to Terminator)
- AI will take our jobs

To address these philistine assumptions, two counter-theses were suggested by the enlightened audience:
- The current state of AI development is too early and immature. Decades will pass before we can achieve any semblance of AGI. So humanity is pretty safe for now. 
- AI will automate mundane and repetitive tasks leaving the creative part to humans, and AI disruption is going to create more jobs than it will make redundant.

Fast-forward to 2026, I am writing this post after being laid off together with other technical writers because someone at the C-level decided AI can handle our job — almost the same week as the ex-Anthropic Jacob Coxon [blew the whistle](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/anthropic-researcher-quits-over-out-of-control-ai-fears-707b7628) that AI bosses genuinely fear their brainchild can kill mankind in the coming decade. Oh, the irony.
